Achilles Rupture Emergency Triad

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Perform the Thompson calf squeeze test in prone.
  2. Carefully palpate the Achilles tendon course 2–6 cm proximal to the calcaneus for a focal gap.
  3. Perform the Matles test: observe prone knee flexion to 90°; inspect resting foot angle.
  4. Note: gap may be obscured by hematoma after 24–48 h; late negative palpation does not exclude rupture.
  5. If ≥ 2 tests positive, immediately immobilize in equinus boot and initiate urgent orthopaedic referral (< 48 h).
Positive Criterion
Presence of ≥ 2 positive findings among Thompson, palpable gap, and Matles test.
Diagnostic Metrics & Evidence
≥ 2 POSITIVE (SN/SP > 98%): Equinus boot + urgent orthopaedic referral (< 48 h). No loading.
Validated References
Maffulli N (1998) · Garras DN et al. (2012)

Budapest Diagnostic Criteria for CRPS

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Verify continuing pain disproportionate to the inciting noxious event.
  2. Assess 4 symptom categories: Sensory (hyperesthesia/allodynia), Vasomotor (temperature/color changes), Sudomotor/Edema (edema/sweating asymmetry), Motor/Trophic (decreased ROM, weakness, tremor, trophic changes).
  3. Assess the same 4 sign categories on physical examination.
  4. Calculate positive categories: diagnostic requires ≥ 3/4 symptom categories and ≥ 2/4 sign categories.
  5. Rule out any other condition that could account for the degree of pain and dysfunction.
Positive Criterion
Reported symptoms in ≥ 3 of 4 categories AND demonstrated signs in ≥ 2 of 4 categories.
Diagnostic Metrics & Evidence
Budapest CRPS [H] (≥ 3/4 Sx, ≥ 2/4 Signs): Sensory · Vasomotor · Edema · Trophic → I / II

Charcot Neuroarthropathy Diagnostic Criteria

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Assess for history of peripheral neuropathy (diabetic, alcoholic, idiopathic).
  2. Measure dermal temperature asymmetry between involved and contralateral limb using infrared thermometry.
  3. Perform the 10-minute elevation test: observe if unilateral erythema significantly reduces.
  4. Palpate pedal pulses (dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial) to confirm adequate vascular supply.
  5. Inquire regarding pain level (paradoxically minimal or absent despite florid clinical inflammation).
Positive Criterion
Temperature difference > 2 °C + erythema resolving on elevation + bounding pulses in neuropathic foot.
Diagnostic Metrics & Evidence
Temp diff > 2 °C · Erythema reduces on 10 min elevation · Minimal/absent pain · Palpable pulses present → IMMEDIATE total-contact offloading + urgent referral. NO loading. NO manual therapy. NO ESWT.
Validated References
Rogers LC et al. (2011) · Frykberg RG et al. (2006)

Compartment Syndrome — 6 Ps & ΔP Threshold

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Screen for the 6 Ps: Pain out of proportion / on passive stretch, Paresthesia, Pallor, Paralysis, Pulselessness, Poikilothermia.
  2. Note that pain on passive stretch is the earliest and most sensitive clinical sign.
  3. Measure diastolic blood pressure (mmHg).
  4. Measure direct intracompartmental pressure using needle manometry.
  5. Calculate ΔP = Diastolic BP − Compartment Pressure.
  6. Pressure measurement is a medical procedure. PT role: recognise and refer immediately.
Positive Criterion
ΔP ≤ 30 mmHg indicates critical tissue hypoperfusion requiring EMERGENCY FASCIOTOMY.
Diagnostic Metrics & Evidence
ΔP (Diastolic BP − Compartment Pressure) = ___ mmHg → ≤ 30 mmHg = EMERGENCY FASCIOTOMY INDICATED
Validated References
McQueen MM, Court-Brown CM (1996) · von Keudell AG et al. (2015)

Lisfranc Tarsometatarsal Joint Assessment

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Inspect plantar midfoot for hallmark ecchymosis ('bruise sign').
  2. Stabilize hindfoot with one hand, grasp metatarsal head with opposite hand.
  3. Apply alternating dorsal and plantar passive stress (piano-key test).
  4. Palpate 1st–2nd metatarsal base interspace for diastasis and focal tenderness.
  5. Perform passive pronation-abduction stress across the midfoot.
Positive Criterion
Severe focal midfoot pain, palpable diastasis, or gross instability on piano-key testing.
Diagnostic Metrics & Evidence
High-Risk Fracture Checklist [L*] — Plantar ecchymosis · Piano-key · 1st–2nd MT diastasis

Ottawa Ankle & Midfoot Rules & Exclusions

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Screen for exclusion criteria: age < 5, intoxication, altered mental status, distracting injury, gross swelling, neuropathy, presentation > 10 days post-injury.
  2. Palpate posterior edge and tip of lateral malleolus (distal 6 cm).
  3. Palpate posterior edge and tip of medial malleolus (distal 6 cm).
  4. Assess ability to bear weight for 4 consecutive steps immediately and at assessment.
  5. For midfoot: palpate base of 5th metatarsal and navicular bone; assess 4-step weight bearing.
Positive Criterion
Bony tenderness at specified landmarks OR inability to bear weight 4 steps mandates radiography.
Diagnostic Metrics & Evidence
Malleolar Zone → Ankle Radiograph [H] SN 98% · Midfoot Zone → Foot Radiograph [H] SN 98%

Thompson Test (Calf Squeeze Test)

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Position the patient in prone with feet hanging freely over the table edge (or kneeling on a chair).
  2. Ensure the calf and ankle are completely relaxed.
  3. Firmly squeeze the muscular bulk of the middle third of the calf with your palm and fingers.
  4. Observe the foot for passive ankle plantarflexion.
  5. Compare bilaterally with the unaffected limb.
Positive Criterion
Absence of passive ankle plantarflexion upon calf compression indicates complete Achilles rupture.
Diagnostic Metrics & Evidence
Achilles Rupture Emergency Triad: Thompson · Palpable Gap (2–6 cm) · Matles Test (prone 90°) — ≥ 2 POSITIVE (SN/SP > 98%)

Dorsiflexion-Eversion Test & Tinel for Tarsal Tunnel

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Position the patient in supine or seated with the ankle relaxed.
  2. Passively take the ankle into maximal dorsiflexion and maximal eversion, and extend all toes.
  3. Hold this end-range tension for 10–15 seconds to compress the tibial nerve within the flexor retinaculum.
  4. Gently tap (percuss) the tibial nerve posterior and inferior to the medial malleolus.
  5. Inquire regarding distal paresthesia or radiating burning into the plantar foot.

Reproduction of radiating numbness, paresthesia, or pain along the plantar nerve distribution (Tinel positive).

PTTD Staging & Too-Many-Toes Sign

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Inspect the patient standing from directly behind.
  2. Evaluate hindfoot alignment (valgus deviation) and observe forefoot abduction.
  3. Count visible lateral toes: more than 1.5–2 toes lateral to the calcaneus indicates positive Too-Many-Toes sign.

Forefoot abduction (> 1.5–2 toes visible laterally), failure of calcaneal inversion on heel raise, or rigid deformity.

Straight Leg Raise (SLR) & Neurodynamic Screening

▼
Execution Steps
  1. Patient supine with head flat and legs relaxed in extension.
  2. Grasp patient's heel and elevate the lower extremity while maintaining full knee extension.
  3. Continuously monitor for symptom reproduction and note the hip flexion angle (positive 35–70°).
  4. Perform structural differentiation: lower leg slightly and add ankle dorsiflexion or neck flexion.
  5. Observe whether distal symptoms increase with distal/cervical neural sensitization.
Positive Criterion
