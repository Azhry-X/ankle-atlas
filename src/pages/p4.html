JOSPT LAS CPG — diagnosis (Martin 2021 J Orthop Sports Phys Ther) (Guideline): “Clinicians should use special tests, including the reverse anterolateral drawer test and anterolateral talar palpation in addition to the traditional anterior drawer test” (B) · CAI: use a discriminative instrument “such as the Cumberland Ankle Instability Tool or the Identification of Functional Ankle Instability” plus functional performance tests (B)
JOSPT Achilles CPG — diagnosis (Martin 2018 J Orthop Sports Phys Ther) (Guideline): in addition to the arc sign and Royal London Hospital test: “pain located 2 to 6 cm proximal to the Achilles tendon insertion that began gradually and pain with palpation of the midportion of the tendon” (C)

Outcome Scale Scoring Rule & Subscales Reliability, MDC, MCID & Cut-Offs Initial Follow-Up
FAAM Calc 21-item ADL (0–84) and 8-item Sports (0–32) subscales → 0–100% Test–retest 0.89 / 0.87; MDC ±5.7 (ADL) and ±12.3 (Sports) (Martin 2005) M
CAIT Calc 9 items, scored per ankle (0–30; lower score = greater instability) Original threshold 27.5 (Sn 82.9%, Sp 74.7%), ICC .96 (Hiller 2006); recalibrated ≤25 for CAI: Sn 96.6%, Sp 86.8% (Wright 2014) M
VISA-A Calc 8 items (stiffness, pain, function, sport; 0–100) Test–retest r = 0.93; controls exceeded 96 (Robinson 2001) M
LEFS Calc 20 lower-extremity activities (0–4 each; total 0–80) Test–retest R = .94; MDC 9 and MCID 9 points (Binkley 1999) M
ATRS Patient-reported outcome after Achilles tendon rupture Validated patient-reported score after Achilles rupture (Nilsson-Helander 2007) M
NPRS Calc · PSFS Calc NPRS 0–10 (now / best / worst) · PSFS mean of 3–5 patient goals (0–10) Pain intensity & patient-specific goal tracking E
